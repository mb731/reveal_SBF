God's Test of Job

Job 2:1-10

Context

The book of Job is unique in Scripture in that it lacks many of the attachments other biblical books have to historical events, people, and places.

Main Idea: Every circumstance in our lives is an opportunity to respond with faith in God.

Timeline

  • Satan's First Test (Job 1)

  • The Lord Speaks to Job (Job 38-41)
  • The Lord Restores Family and Possesions to Job (Job 42)

Satan is Powerful But Has Limits in The World

(Job 2:1-6)

It might seem shocking, but upon receiving all this earth-shattering news, Job responded in light of his faith in God. He tore his robe, shaved his head, and fell to the ground in reverence—Job grieved, and he worshiped.

Why (How) can grief and worship coexist in the life of a believer?

God’s purposes for doing so are not evident from the outset, yet Job’s suffering would ultimately serve to glorify God as One who both comforts His people and sovereignly orchestrates all things toward good and God-glorifying ends.

God is Sovereign Over All of Our Circumstances

(Job 2:7-10)

We think it should feel easier to worship God when everything in our lives is going well, yet we should remember God is glorified in our dependence on Him, especially amid life’s difficulties.

What encouragement do you find from Job’s example to maintain his integrity and his faith in God?
We do not need to convince ourselves that pain and suffering are good in and of themselves.
What does your response to suffering reveal about your faith in God?
